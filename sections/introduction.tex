\section{Introduction}

Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platforms have become integral to personal and professional productivity workflows, serving millions of users across diverse organizational contexts. Among contemporary productivity tools, Notion has established significant market presence through its modular architecture and continuous feature expansion, including recent integration of artificial intelligence capabilities. However, as such platforms evolve, product teams face increasingly complex decisions regarding feature development prioritization. Reliance on intuition or reactive feedback mechanisms proves insufficient in competitive markets where development resources must be allocated strategically.

Demand forecasting presents a systematic alternative to reactive approaches. Rather than responding to user complaints or competitor actions post-hoc, forecasting methodologies analyze observable signals—search behavior patterns, sentiment in user reviews, and temporal shifts in feature interest—to predict which capabilities will generate the greatest user value in subsequent periods. For SaaS products, this forecasting challenge differs substantially from traditional retail demand prediction. Software feature interest does not conform to predictable seasonal patterns and may fluctuate rapidly in response to viral content, competitive announcements, or shifts in work practices.

This study examines how demand forecasting can inform product design decisions, utilizing Notion as an empirical case study. The investigation focuses exclusively on publicly available data, as proprietary internal analytics from commercial software companies remain inaccessible to external researchers. Through analysis of Google Trends search interest data, app store review corpora, and user feedback patterns, this work demonstrates how forecasting models can identify features with increasing demand trajectories and those exhibiting declining interest. The objective extends beyond prediction accuracy to establishing connections between forecasted demand and actionable design decisions, including user interface improvements, priority reordering, and release timing optimization.

\subsection{Research Motivation}
SaaS productivity platforms operate within a continuously evolving competitive landscape. User expectations change rapidly, novel workflow patterns emerge frequently, and competitive pressure remains constant. Accurate timing of feature development and release can significantly impact product success. Given the inaccessibility of proprietary analytics, this research develops a forecasting methodology utilizing publicly available proxies: Google Trends for search interest quantification, Kaggle datasets for review analysis, and app metadata for ratings and installation metrics. While these proxies have inherent limitations, they provide a practical foundation for teams seeking to incorporate empirical data into design processes.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
Product teams at SaaS organizations face persistent prioritization challenges. Determining which features warrant improvement and which new capabilities should receive development resources requires systematic analysis. Without comprehensive analytics infrastructure, teams frequently default to intuition-based decisions or respond disproportionately to vocal user segments. A data-driven methodology that anticipates user requirements and integrates predictive insights into design decisions addresses a significant gap in current practice.

\subsection{Research Objectives}
This research pursues four primary objectives: first, to characterize temporal patterns in Notion feature interest through analysis of publicly available data including Google Trends and user reviews; second, to apply established forecasting methodologies—Exponential Smoothing, ARIMA, and Regression analysis—to predict feature demand trajectories; third, to explore how these predictions can inform product decisions regarding prioritization, improvement, and strategic positioning; and fourth, to develop a practical framework enabling software teams to connect demand insights with UI/UX design and release planning.

\subsection{Scope and Significance}
This framework does not claim to address all forecasting challenges within the SaaS domain. However, it demonstrates that useful demand predictions can be constructed from publicly available data without proprietary analytics infrastructure. Effective forecasting enables organizations to develop features aligned with user requirements, maintain competitive positioning, reduce user attrition, and improve adoption rates. While Notion serves as the primary case study, the methodology is designed for adaptation to other SaaS platforms.
