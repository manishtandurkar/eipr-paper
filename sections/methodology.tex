\section{Methodology}

The methodological framework employed in this study is structured into four distinct, sequential phases: data acquisition, preprocessing and transformation, predictive model development, and the derivation of actionable design recommendations. This multi-stage approach ensures that raw, unstructured public data is systematically converted into reliable, quantitative insights suitable for guiding product strategy.

\subsection{Data Acquisition}
Due to the proprietary nature of internal application analytics, this research necessitates the utilization of public proxy metrics to estimate feature demand. The data acquisition strategy encompasses three primary channels. First, search interest indices are harvested from Google Trends, serving as a proxy for user intent and feature awareness. Specific query terms (e.g., ``Notion AI'', ``Notion templates'') were monitored to quantify temporal fluctuations in interest. Second, user feedback is aggregated from Kaggle-hosted datasets containing app store reviews, providing a rich corpus for qualitative sentiment extraction. Third, application metadata—including version history, cumulative download statistics, and rating distributions—is collected to provide contextual baselines for observing demand shifts.

The acquisition infrastructure includes robust mechanisms for rate-limiting mitigation and error handling to ensure data completeness. All acquired datasets are timestamped and harmonized to a monthly temporal resolution to facilitate cross-channel correlation.

\subsection{Data Preprocessing and Transformation}
Raw data artifacts undergo accurate preprocessing to ensure analytical validity. The cleaning process standardizes the dataset by identifying and removing duplicate records to prevent frequency distortion. Missing values are addressed through forward-fill imputation, prioritizing temporal continuity. Statistical outliers, defined as deviations exceeding three standard deviations from the rolling mean, are flagged for review.

To extract quantitative signals from unstructured text, Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques are applied to the user review corpus. This process involves tokenization and sentiment polarity scoring, classifying user affect regarding specific features as positive, negative, or neutral. These sentiment scores are subsequently aggregated into monthly time-series indices.

\subsection{Predictive Modeling Framework}
To mitigate the risk of single-model bias, this study employs a composite forecasting strategy utilizing three complementary statistical approaches \cite{makridakis2018statistical}:

\subsubsection{Exponential Smoothing (ETS)}
Selected for its responsiveness to recent data, Exponential Smoothing applies weighted averages where weights decay exponentially over time. This method is particularly effective for capturing immediate trend shifts \cite{hyndman2008forecasting}.

\subsubsection{Auto-Regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA)}
The ARIMA methodology is utilized to model temporal dependencies, identifying stationarity, seasonality, and autocorrelation structures within the data. This model provides robust forecasting capability for longer-term cyclical patterns \cite{box2015time}.

\subsubsection{Linear Regression with Exogenous Variables}
Linear regression is employed to quantify the impact of external factors. This approach models the relationship between feature demand and independent variables such as competitive product launches and industry-wide trends.

\subsubsection{Ensemble Aggregation}
The outputs of these individual models are synthesized using a performance-weighted ensemble technique. Weights are dynamically assigned based on the inverse of the historical Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of each model, ensuring that the final forecast prioritizes the most accurate predictor for each specific time horizon.

\subsection{Decision Support and Recommendation Logic}
The quantitative forecasts are translated into qualitative strategic recommendations through a rule-based decision logic. Features exhibiting a statistically significant upward trend are prioritized for immediate development resource allocation and user interface optimization. Stable features are designated for maintenance and incremental improvement. Conversely, features demonstrating a consistent negative trajectory trigger a recommendation for strategic re-evaluation or deprecation. This translation layer ensures that statistical findings result in concrete, actionable product decisions.

\subsection{Performance Evaluation Protocol}
Model performance is rigorously assessed using a temporal train-test split strategy. To simulate real-world forecasting conditions, a rolling-origin cross-validation approach is employed. The accuracy of predictions is quantified using standard metrics: Mean Absolute Error (MAE) to measure average magnitude of errors, Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) to penalize large deviations, and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) to facilitate comparison across variable scales.

Where analytically feasible, 95\% confidence intervals are computed for all forecasts. These intervals provide decision-makers with a quantification of uncertainty, delineating the probabilistic bounds within which true future values are expected to lie.

\subsection{Reproducibility Summary}
To facilitate peer verification and future extension of this work, a comprehensive reproducibility package has been developed. This includes all data acquisition scripts, preprocessing pipelines, model specifications, and visualization code, made available in the project repository. The documentation ensures that the methodology can be replicated on equivalent datasets or adapted for alternative SaaS product contexts.
