Every product team faces the same challenge: figuring out what to build next. Most end up relying on gut instinct or waiting until users start complaining, but in a fast-moving market where competitors never sleep, that reactive approach rarely works out well. We wanted to see if there's a better way—one that could actually predict which features will gain traction before teams pour months of effort into building them.

We chose Notion as our testing ground since it's popular enough to generate substantial public discussion. Of course, Notion isn't about to hand over their internal analytics to researchers, so we had to get resourceful. We tapped into Google Trends data, combed through app store reviews, and tracked user feedback patterns—all information that anyone can access without special permissions.

Our forecasting approach doesn't rely on any single method. Exponential Smoothing helps us catch sudden spikes in interest. ARIMA reveals the longer-term ebb and flow of user demand. Regression analysis lets us factor in external events, like when a competitor drops a new feature or something goes viral on social media. Blending these techniques gives us a much richer picture than betting everything on one model.

The results turned out pretty promising. Looking at features like Notion's AI tools, templates, and automation capabilities, we found genuine patterns that translate into actionable guidance. When we see AI interest climbing month over month, that's a signal to double down on development. When templates show rock-solid consistency, the message is clear: keep them working well and don't break what users love.

Perhaps the most important finding is that you don't need deep pockets or insider connections to forecast demand intelligently. Armed with publicly available data and some solid analytical methods, even lean teams can make smarter prioritization calls, waste less time on features nobody wants, and ship products that actually resonate with users.
