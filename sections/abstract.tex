\section{Abstract}
In today's competitive Software-as-a-Service landscape, product teams face the ongoing challenge of prioritizing features that align with evolving user needs. This study presents a data-driven approach to demand forecasting for SaaS platforms, using Notion as a case study to demonstrate how publicly available data can inform strategic design decisions. Traditional feature prioritization methods often rely on developer intuition or limited user feedback, which prove insufficient in rapidly changing markets. We address this gap by developing a forecasting framework that analyzes publicly accessible proxy datasets, including Google Trends data, app reviews, and user feedback patterns, to predict feature demand trajectories. Our methodology employs multiple forecasting techniques—Exponential Smoothing, ARIMA, and Regression models—to capture both linear and nonlinear demand patterns influenced by competitor launches, viral trends, and shifting work habits. The research focuses on forecasting demand for key Notion features such as AI capabilities, templates, and automation workflows. By demonstrating how demand predictions can guide UI/UX improvements, workflow design, and release planning, this study establishes a practical framework that SaaS companies can adopt even without extensive internal analytics. The results show that integrating demand forecasting into the product development cycle enables teams to create more relevant, intuitive products while potentially reducing user churn and improving adoption rates.
