Software product teams face a persistent challenge in determining feature prioritization strategies. Traditional approaches often rely on intuition or reactive feedback mechanisms, which prove inadequate in competitive markets characterized by rapid technological evolution. This study investigates whether predictive demand forecasting methodologies can provide actionable guidance for product design decisions prior to resource allocation.

Notion, a prominent SaaS productivity platform, serves as the empirical case study for this research. Given the unavailability of proprietary internal analytics, this investigation leverages publicly accessible data sources including Google Trends search interest data, app store review corpora, and user feedback patterns to construct demand signals for specific product features.

The proposed forecasting framework employs an ensemble methodology integrating multiple analytical techniques. Exponential Smoothing models capture short-term fluctuations in user interest. ARIMA analysis reveals longer-term cyclical patterns and seasonality in demand trajectories. Regression analysis incorporates external factors such as competitive landscape changes and market events. The combination of these techniques provides a more comprehensive demand signal than any individual method.

Empirical results demonstrate discernible patterns across analyzed features including AI-powered capabilities, template functionality, and automation workflows. AI-related features exhibit sustained growth trajectories, templates display consistent baseline demand with predictable seasonal variations, and automation capabilities show moderate growth correlated with broader industry trends in workflow optimization.

The primary contribution of this work is demonstrating that meaningful demand forecasting for software feature prioritization is achievable using exclusively publicly available information. Organizations with limited analytical infrastructure can implement data-driven prioritization methodologies to reduce misallocation of development resources and improve alignment between product roadmaps and user requirements.
