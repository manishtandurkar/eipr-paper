\section{Conclusion}

This research demonstrates that data-driven demand forecasting can serve as a strategic foundation for feature prioritization in SaaS products, even when internal analytics are unavailable. By focusing on Notion as a representative case study, we developed and validated a forecasting framework that leverages publicly accessible datasets—including Google Trends, app reviews, and user feedback patterns—to predict feature demand trajectories with meaningful accuracy.

The study successfully achieved its core objectives. We established that search interest patterns correlate with actual feature adoption, validated multiple forecasting techniques (Exponential Smoothing, ARIMA, and Regression models) against real-world data, and demonstrated how these predictions can inform concrete product decisions. Our methodology revealed distinct demand patterns for different feature categories: AI capabilities showing exponential growth curves, templates exhibiting steady seasonal patterns, and automation workflows displaying sensitivity to competitor movements and workflow trends.

Beyond technical forecasting accuracy, this work contributes a practical framework that product teams can implement without significant infrastructure investment. The approach democratizes sophisticated analytics, making data-driven product management accessible to startups and smaller teams that lack extensive data science resources. By bridging the gap between forecasting theory and actionable product insights, we show that demand prediction is not merely an academic exercise but a valuable tool for creating products that remain relevant and competitive.

The implications extend beyond Notion to the broader SaaS ecosystem. As digital products become increasingly complex and user expectations continue to evolve rapidly, the ability to anticipate demand shifts becomes crucial for product success. Our framework provides a replicable methodology that can be adapted across platforms, feature types, and market segments, enabling more informed decisions about UI/UX investments, engineering priorities, and release timing.

While challenges remain—particularly in capturing sudden trend shifts and accounting for competitive dynamics—the research establishes that publicly available data contains sufficient signal to guide strategic product decisions. The integration of multiple forecasting techniques, combined with domain knowledge about SaaS adoption patterns, creates a robust approach that balances statistical rigor with practical applicability.

Ultimately, this study reinforces a fundamental principle: understanding future user needs is as important as understanding current behaviors. By incorporating demand forecasting into the product development lifecycle, SaaS companies can transition from reactive feature development to proactive design strategies, creating experiences that anticipate rather than merely respond to user demands. This shift has the potential to reduce development waste, improve user satisfaction, and strengthen product-market fit in an increasingly competitive landscape.
